Vocal lines

Give each voice a line of its own, starting where the section starts. Write the notes in order with their length in beats after a colon: E4:2 holds for two beats, F#4:1/2 for half a beat, a bare note for one, and r:1 rests for a beat. Name the first note’s octave; the others find the nearest one.

Or draw it: click empty space to write a note for the voice whose ✎ Draw is lit, dragging right to lengthen it. Drag a note to move it, drag its right edge to resize it, double-click or right-click to erase it. How-to

    
Short guides to the parts of the tome you'll use most. Everything here works on the song open on the Composition page. Tap a guide to open it.

    


    
Start here: your first song in five minutes
      
    
        
  1. Start a song. On Composition, press New song and type a title. It saves as you go.
    2. 
        
  2. Type the chords of one section. In Chords, write them the way you'd say them: Am F C G. Spaces or commas between chords both work.
    3. 
        
  3. Press ▶ Play section. The band plays them with a click. Tick or untick Guitar, Bass and Piano under Band playback to choose who plays.
    4. 
        
  4. Add the next section. Use + Add section… next to the section tabs (Chorus, Bridge and so on) and type its chords.
    5. 
        
  5. Put the song in order. In Song map, add sections to the running order, then press ▶ Play song.
    6. 
      

      
Every other page (Chord explorer, Progression path, Soloing scales, Tunings) shows the section you're working on. Change section from the Your song strip at the top of any page.

    


    
Writing chords: lengths, rests and tricky chords
      
    
        
  • Chord lengths. Each chord lasts one bar. To change it, tap the chord tile and pick a length, or type it in: Am:2 is two bars, G:1/2 is half a bar.
    • 
        
  • Rests. Type N.C. for a bar where nobody plays.
    • 
        
  • Any chord name works: F#m7b5, Cmaj9, E7(b9,#11), Am/C (A minor with C in the bass).
    • 
        
  • The key is found for you. The line under the chords says the key and how sure it is. To set it yourself, open Section settings and choose the Home note and Mode.
    • 
        
  • Chord tiles explain each chord: its number in the key (like ♭VI), whether it's in the key or borrowed, and its job (home, moving away, pulling back home).
    • 
        
  • Section settings (folded under the chords) hold the section's name, key, its own tempo and time signature if it changes, and whether it loops back to the start.
    • 
      

    


    
Playback: loops, starting mid-song, and watching it play
      
    
        
  • ▶ Play section plays the section you're looking at. With Loop on, it repeats.
    • 
        
  • ▶ From here. Tap a chord tile, then press ▶ From [chord] to start there. Long-press (or shift-click) a second chord to stop there instead of at the end. With Loop on, just that stretch repeats.
    • 
        
  • Change chords while it loops. Swap or retype a chord and the next time round plays the new version, without stopping.
    • 
        
  • ▶ Play song plays the running order. ▶ Song from here starts at the section you're on, and the ▶ on each block in the running order starts at that exact spot.
    • 
        
  • Watch it play shows what your instrument is playing as it plays: the guitar shape, the bass note or the piano keys.
    • 
        
  • Click and Count-in give you a metronome, plus one bar of clicks before the band starts.
    • 
        
  • The play bar at the bottom follows you to every page, so you can start, stop or play from a chord while you're on the Chord explorer or Soloing scales. On the Composition page it appears once you scroll past the main buttons. Tap ▾ to shrink it to just play/stop; ▴ brings the rest back.
    • 
      

    


    
Keyboard shortcuts (computer)
      
    
        
  • Space plays or stops the section. Shift+Space plays or stops the song.
    • 
        
  • ← → step through the chords in the section and play each one.
    • 
        
  • F plays from the selected chord. L turns the section loop on or off.
    • 
        
  • Esc stops playback, or clears the selected chord.
    • 
        
  • ? opens this list.
    • 
        
  • Shortcuts are ignored while you're typing in a box. Space doesn't press a focused button; use Enter for that.
    • 
      

    


    
Chord lab: swapping chords and borrowing colour
      
    
        
  1. Tap a chord tile to select it.
    2. 
        
  2. Under Swap it for, tap an option to hear it in place. Tapping puts it in your section; Undo takes it back.
    3. 
        
  3. Lead into it adds a chord before the one you selected, and Follow it with adds one after. The new chord becomes the selection, so you can keep writing forward.
    4. 
        
  4. Open the Modal interchange palette for borrowed chords: chords from the key's parallel major or minor and its modes that add darker or brighter colour. Each card says how smoothly it connects to the chords around it.
    5. 
        
  5. Reharmonize this section (folded further down) offers whole new versions: Darker, Brighter, Jazzier, Simpler or Pop/EDM.
    6. 
      

    


    
Song map: running order, timing and how sections connect
      
    
        
  • Add sections to the running order with the + buttons; move them with ‹ › or remove them with ×.
    • 
        
  • Each block shows its key, start time, length and bar number. The total time is underneath.
    • 
        
  • How the sections connect explains each change of section: whether the key changes, which chord hands over to which, and the chords the sections share.
    • 
      

    


    
Chord explorer: every way to play a chord
      
    
        
  • The Chords in [section] row at the top shows your section's chords: tap one to explore it. Or type any chord in the search box, or build one from Root and the type buttons.
    • 
        
  • The neck map shows every note of the chord, coloured by role: red root, blue 3rd, green 5th, violet 7th. Below it, every shape: choose a Voicing family. CAGED holds the familiar open and barre shapes; Close triads, Drop 2 and Drop 3 hold the shapes players use for movement and colour. String set and Bass note narrow the list.The 4-string neck shows every chord tone, and the arpeggio boxes show the chord's notes within one hand position. Tap a box to hear it.The keyboard shows every chord tone, and the voicing cards show the shapes pianists actually use: close positions, inversions, shells, open tenths and rootless voicings. Rings mark the left hand.
    • 
        
  • Tap any shape to hear it.
    • 
        
  • For chords with five or more notes, Notes in the shape lets you choose which four notes the shape keeps.
    • 
      

    


    
Progression path: playing your section smoothly
      
    
        
  • The page shows your current section's chords. Change section in the Your song strip.
    • 
        
  • Fret zone keeps every shape inside the frets you pick (5–9, say), and the number between chords counts the notes that stay put. Fewer moving notes means smoother changes. Bass note and Top 4 strings only narrow the choices.
    • 
        
  • Choose a Bass line style (Roots, Root and 5th, approach notes, walking line). The line follows your section's chord lengths and time signature. Show as switches between the Fretboard, where each note is coloured by its role in the chord and lights up as it plays, and Tab.
    • 
        
  • The left hand plays the root, and the right hand moves to the nearest inversion each time. The number between chords counts the notes that stay.
    • 
        
  • Trying something? Type over the chords to test other changes. A bar appears saying you're previewing: Back to [section] drops the test, and Replace [section] progression writes them into your song. A ✓ note confirms it, with Undo.
    • 
      

    


    
Soloing scales: what to play over your section
      
    
        
  • The top three cards are the scales that fit your section best. The chips under each one say whether it fits, rubs or clashes with each chord, and the notes explain how to handle the rubs.
    • 
        
  • Home note starts on your section's key. Pick another to see the scales built on it.
    • 
        
  • Or follow the changes gives one pentatonic per chord, for when you want to move with the chords instead of playing one scale over everything.
    • 
        
  • Show target notes over lights up a chord's strongest notes on the map, so you can aim your phrases at them.
    • 
        
  • Tap any key on the keyboard to hear it. When you tap a scale card, each key lights up as its note plays.
    • 
      

    


    
Chord finder: naming a shape you stumbled on
      
    
        
  1. Tap the neck where your fingers are, one note per string. Tap left of the nut for an open string, and tap a note again to lift it.Tap the keys you're holding. The keyboard shows three octaves; ‹ Octave down and Octave up › slide it. Notes you've tapped stay in the chord when they slide out of view, and a note beside the buttons says how many are hidden.

  2. The name appears at the top, with other names for the same notes underneath.
    3. 
        
  3. Add to this section puts the chord at the end of the section you're working on. Show this chord in the explorer shows every other way to play it.

Alternate tunings: writing a song in DADGAD, Open G and the rest
      
    
        
  • On the Chord finder & tuning page, pick a tuning (DADGAD, Open G and so on), or make your own with − and + on each string.
    • 
        
  • The tuning is saved with the song. Other songs keep their own tuning; a new song starts in standard.
    • 
        
  • When a song isn't in standard tuning, every guitar page uses its tuning: the chord finder names shapes in it, the explorer, Progression path, capo helper and playback use shapes that work in it, and the scale maps show its open strings. The Your song strip shows the tuning so you always know.
    • 
        
  • In the explorer, In this tuning lists shapes that let open strings ring. CAGED and Root on 6th are hidden in other tunings because their grips only work in standard.
    • 
        
  • Your section in this tuning, lower on the page, gives each chord the shape where the most open strings ring. Use ‹ › to step through others, and Play through to hear the section.
    • 
      

    


    
Transpose, capo and the chord sheet
      
    
        
  • Transpose, capo and tuning (under the song title) moves the whole song up or down, chords and lyrics together. The capo helper's list marks the positions that give easy open shapes with ★.
    • 
        
  • Playing with a capo. Pick a capo position and each section's shapes appear as diagrams; tap one to hear it. Playback, Watch it play (which draws the capo on the neck) and Progression path then show the shapes you fret above the capo, while everything still sounds in the song's key. Set it back to No capo to turn it off. The song remembers its capo.
    • 
        
  • Lyrics (folded near the bottom of each section): put each chord in brackets just before its syllable, like [Am]Words go [F]here. The chart previews beside it.
    • 
        
  • Song ▾ → Chord chart & lead sheet opens a printable page. Chord chart shows each section as a grid of bars (% means repeat the previous bar), with lyrics. Lead sheet (staff) writes the song out in its running order on music staff: chord symbols over rhythm slashes, with key, time signature, tempo and section marks. Print prints it; Save page keeps a copy you can print later.
    • 
        
  • Song ▾ → Export MIDI + MusicXML saves a .zip. The .mid goes straight into Pro Tools or any DAW, with tempo and time changes, section markers, and three tracks: chords (piano voicing), chords (guitar voicing) and the bass line. The .musicxml opens in MuseScore, Sibelius, Dorico or Finale as an editable lead sheet.
    • 
        
  • Song ▾ → Export chord sheet (text) gives you the whole song, with chords, lyrics and section tempos, as plain text.
    • 
      

    


    
Keeping your songs safe
      
    
        
  • Songs save automatically as you type.
    • 
        
  • Song ▾ → Back up all songs saves every song to one file. Do it now and then, and before big changes.
    • 
        
  • Song ▾ → Restore from a backup… loads that file. If a song already exists, the tome asks whether to replace it or keep both.
    • 
        
  • Song ▾ → Delete this song asks twice before it deletes.

Practice routines
      

        
Triads on the top strings
In Chord explorer, choose Close triads and the 4-3-2 or 3-2-1 string set. Small shapes there leave fingers free for fills, which is where Hendrix, Mayfield and Mayer rhythm parts live.

        
Stay in one zone
In Progression path, pick a fret zone like 5–9 and play your section with only those shapes. Then move the zone up and repeat. Loop the section on Composition to play along.

        
Land on the 3rd or 7th
End each fill on a blue (3rd) or violet (7th) note of the next chord. Turn on Watch it play to see where they are as the chords change.

        
Loop the hard part
Select the chord before a tricky change and press ▶ From here with Loop on. Lower the song tempo while you learn it, then bring it back up.

      

      

        
Roots first
Play your section with Bass style on Roots, locked to the click. Then move to Root and 5th, then approach notes, then walking. Only move on when the simpler one feels easy.

        
One hand position
Use the arpeggio boxes in Chord explorer to play every chord without moving your hand more than a fret or two.

        
Watch the line
Turn on Watch it play and follow the bass line note by note, then play it with the band muted.

      

      

        
Inversions that barely move
Play Progression path's right-hand shapes: most notes stay put between chords. Say the number of moving notes out loud as you play.

        
Shells, then colour
Play root and 7th in the left hand and 3rd and 5th in the right. Then try the rootless voicings in Chord explorer.

        
Watch the keys
Loop your section with Watch it play on and copy the lit keys until you can play it without looking.
